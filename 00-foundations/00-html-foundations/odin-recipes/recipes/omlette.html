Omlette

Description

An omelette is a quick, protein-rich dish made by whisking eggs together and cooking them in a buttered or oiled skillet until set, then folding them around savory fillings.

Light, tender, and endlessly customizable, it is a staple breakfast or brunch meal that pairs well with toast, fresh greens, or roasted potatoes.

Ingredients

  • Eggs. 2 to 3 large eggs, at room temperature for the best texture.
  • Butter or Oil. 1 tablespoon of unsalted butter (or neutral cooking oil).
  • Milk or Water. 1 tablespoon of whole milk or water (optional, for extra tenderness).
  • Seasonings. A pinch of salt and freshly ground black pepper.
  • Fillings. ¼ cup of shredded cheese (such as cheddar, gruyère, or mozzarella), diced vegetables (onions, bell peppers, tomatoes, or spinach), or cooked meats (optional).
  • Garnish. Chopped fresh chives, cilantro, or parsley (optional).

Steps

  1. Whisk the eggs. Crack the eggs into a bowl, add the optional splash of milk or water, salt, and pepper, and beat briskly with a fork or whisk until the yolks and whites are completely blended and slightly frothy.
  2. Melt the butter. Place an 8- or 9-inch nonstick skillet over medium-low heat and add the butter, swirling the pan until it melts and foams without browning.
  3. Pour and set the eggs. Pour the egg mixture into the warm pan. Let it cook undisturbed for about 30 seconds until the edges begin to set, then use a rubber spatula to gently push the cooked edges toward the center while tilting the pan so the raw egg flows underneath.
  4. Add the fillings. Once the surface is mostly set with just a slight sheen on top (about 1 to 2 minutes total), sprinkle your cheese, herbs, or pre-cooked fillings over one half of the omelette.
  5. Fold and serve. Slide your spatula under the unfilled side, fold it gently over the fillings, and let it cook for another 15–20 seconds to melt the cheese. Slide the omelette onto a warm plate, garnish with fresh herbs, and serve immediately.
